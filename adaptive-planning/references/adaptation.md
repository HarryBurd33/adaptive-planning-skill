# Replanning and evidence

## Minimal-change repair

1. Read current revision and new facts; establish the effective date/time. If a tool cannot verify “now,” request the material missing time or label the supplied time.
2. Apply reported progress once. Preserve actual history and elapsed portions; split an in-progress block into elapsed and remaining parts if useful.
3. Identify direct collisions and dependency/deadline consequences. Keep unaffected future blocks when feasible. A temporary exception changes the dated plan only.
4. Search nearby eligible gaps before reshuffling the week. Respect minimum sessions, transitions and the user's priorities.
5. Recompute effort, recurrence counts and capacity. Missed optional habits may lapse without debt. Required work cannot disappear.
6. Show `ID | Before | After | Reason`, or a short equivalent. Quantify unmet work and ask for the consequential decision if repair is infeasible. Save only against the revision read.

Example: a Wed 16:00–17:30 meeting displaces a 16:00 lifting session. Preserve Tuesday's completed paper work and Thursday's unaffected study block. Move that lifting occurrence to an available later window if feasible, keeping its identity. Do not schedule the completed paper work again or rewrite the normal model week.

## What counts as evidence

Explicit instructions take precedence over inferred patterns. Store what was reported, not a guessed cause. A missed session with no explanation gives no evidence of exhaustion. A one-off exam interruption supports a dated exception. Repeated exhaustion at the same time, with successful alternatives, supports a timing trial.

Proposed default for an inferred change: at least three relevant reported observations, a plausible explanation, and no conflict with hard constraints. Use the magnitude and quality of evidence, not a mechanical vote. For duration estimates, compare actual time and completed scope: a partial task cannot be treated as a finished duration sample. Prefer a conservative range when samples vary. For absent tracking, store unknown; do not lower the person's perceived reliability.

Apply reversible changes and explain them when evidence supports it. Record adaptation ID, feedback IDs, target field, old value, new value, reason, effective period and reversal instruction. A small trial can have an explicit review point without scheduling a background check-in. Do not change protected sleep, real deadlines, required scope or fixed commitments by inference. Frequency changes affecting required recurrence need the user's decision; an optional activity can be reduced as a visible reversible trial when the user’s priorities support it.

For “I skipped evening reading three times because I was exhausted. Morning reading worked twice. Keep my wake time unchanged,” move future reading into an available morning window at or after the existing wake time. Explain the five reported observations, protect fixed commitments and retain the evening preference as the reversible prior setting. If no morning capacity exists, report that conflict and offer a concrete alternative instead of waking the user earlier.

To undo, create a new adaptation that restores the prior setting and links the reverted decision; preserve history. Do not delete evidence or claim the model learned permanently. Brief daily check-ins and deeper weekly reviews are optional entry points to the same record, not prerequisites for each other.
